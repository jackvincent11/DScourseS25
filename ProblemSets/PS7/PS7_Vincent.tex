\section*{Analysis of Imputation Methods}

The coefficient $\hat{\beta}_1$ for \texttt{hgc} (highest grade completed) was estimated across four models:

\[
\begin{array}{|l|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Complete Cases} & \textbf{Mean Imputation} & \textbf{Predicted Imputation} & \textbf{MICE} \\
\hline
\hat{\beta}_1 & 0.062 & 0.050 & 0.050 & 0.049 \\
\hline
\end{array}
\]

The true value of $\hat{\beta}_1$ is 0.093. Observations:
- \textbf{Complete case analysis} gives the largest estimate, closer to the true value.
- \textbf{Imputation methods} (mean, predicted, and MICE) all give lower estimates, with very similar results (~0.05).

\subsection*{Patterns in Estimates}
- \textbf{Complete Case} yields the closest estimate to the true value, though with fewer observations (1669).
- \textbf{Imputation methods} generally understate the effect, with mean and predicted imputation showing the smallest estimates.
- \textbf{MICE} provides a robust estimate but still underestimates the true effect slightly.

\subsection*{Veracity of Imputation Methods}
- \textbf{Mean/Predicted Imputation} are biased downward, likely due to oversimplification.
- \textbf{MICE} is more robust but still slightly biased, suggesting the missing data could be \textit{Missing at Random (MAR)}.
